## NASA Data

- **NISAR L2 Geocoded Polarimetric Covariance (GCOV), L-band** — NASA / ISRO, distributed by the Alaska Satellite Facility DAAC. The core of the project: HH and HV backscatter for 10 dates (track 172, frame 168, ascending), classified into open water and flooded vegetation. https://search.asf.alaska.edu/ (Open data (NASA Earth Science Data policy))
- **NASA Global Imagery Browse Services (GIBS): NISAR GCOV browse imagery, OPERA Dynamic Surface Water Extent (DSWx-HLS), MODIS Aqua corrected reflectance** — NASA EOSDIS (OPERA products by NASA JPL). Live reference map layers, shown unmodified next to our own classification. https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/engage/open-data-services-software/earthdata-developer-portal/gibs-api (Open data; NASA asks for acknowledgement of GIBS)
  - We acknowledge the use of imagery provided by services from NASA's Global Imagery Browse Services (GIBS), part of NASA's Earth Science Data and Information System (ESDIS).
- **NASA POWER (MERRA-2) monthly precipitation, PRECTOTCORR** — NASA Langley Research Center. Rainfall in the Angolan headwaters that feeds the flood. https://power.larc.nasa.gov/ (Open data)

## Space Agency Partner Data & References

- **Water Observations from Space (WOfS) annual summary, from USGS/NASA Landsat** — Digital Earth Africa. Open-water extent of every year 1984-2025, the long-term context NISAR cannot give yet. https://docs.digitalearthafrica.org/en/latest/data_specs/Landsat_WOfS_specs.html (CC BY 4.0)
- **JRC Global Surface Water v1.5, occurrence 1984-2024** — European Commission Joint Research Centre. How often each pixel was open water over 40 years. https://global-surface-water.appspot.com/ (Copernicus open licence; cite Pekel et al. 2016, Nature 540)
  - Pekel, J.-F., Cottam, A., Gorelick, N., Belward, A. S. (2016). High-resolution mapping of global surface water and its long-term changes. Nature 540, 418-422.
- **GloFAS river discharge reanalysis** — Copernicus Emergency Management Service / ECMWF, accessed through the Open-Meteo Flood API. Modelled inflow of the Okavango River near Mohembo. https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/flood-api (CC BY 4.0)
- **Base map tiles and data** — OpenStreetMap contributors. Background map. https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright (ODbL)
- Quegan, S., Yu, J. J. (2001). Filtering of multichannel SAR images. IEEE Trans. Geosci. Remote Sens. 39(11), 2373-2379.
- Inman, V. L., Lyons, M. B. (2020). Automated inundation mapping over large areas using Landsat data and Google Earth Engine. Remote Sensing 12(8), 1348.
- Wolski, P., Murray-Hudson, M., Thito, K., Cassidy, L. (2017). Keeping it simple: monitoring flood extent in large data-poor wetlands using MODIS SWIR data. Int. J. Appl. Earth Obs. Geoinf. 57, 224-234.
- NISAR Provisional Product Known Issues, https://nisar-docs.asf.alaska.edu/provisional-known-issues/

## Use of AI

- **Claude (Anthropic), via Claude Code**: Writing and reviewing the Python data pipeline and the serverless assistant function, checking data sources, drafting documentation. Every number in the app is computed by the published pipeline from the cited data, not written by an AI.
- **OpenAI GPT-5.4 mini via API; open-weight gpt-oss models on Groq as backup**: The in-app 'Ask the Delta' assistant. It may only answer from assistant_context.json, a file generated from our data. It is not trained or fine-tuned by us.
- No AI-generated images, video or audio are used. All maps are rendered from satellite data. No NASA logo or insignia is used.
